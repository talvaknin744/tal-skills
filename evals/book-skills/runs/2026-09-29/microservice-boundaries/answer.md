Keep Appointment, Availability, and Room together in the scheduling module and its PostgreSQL transaction. Retain Billing as a separate module in the same application for now. Keep patient management local until its responsibilities and change patterns are understood. **Do not make any of the five proposed service splits now.**

The stated goal is maintainability. The brief supplies no measured contention or release bottleneck: four engineers support 70 clinics at a peak of 18 requests/second, deploy twice weekly through a ten-minute pipeline, and remain one on-call team. These facts do not prove that performance is sufficient, but they provide no evidence that independent scaling or deployment would solve a current problem. Five deployments and databases would add operating and migration work ahead of recurring appointments, due in four weeks, without additional platform or on-call capacity.

The strongest boundary evidence is the booking transaction. Checking availability, creating an appointment, and reserving a room currently happen atomically. Splitting those operations across three databases removes that guarantee. An availability check followed by remote appointment creation is vulnerable to concurrent bookings; asynchronous compensation after accepting two conflicting bookings would not satisfy the clinic requirement. Keep the rules and authoritative state needed to prevent overlap under one owner. The exact scope of “never overlap”—clinician, room, patient, and recurring occurrences—must be specified before designing recurrence; the brief does not resolve those dimensions.

| Proposed home | Responsibility and proposed internal contract | Authoritative state and ownership |
|---|---|---|
| Scheduling module, current application | Accept or reject a booking as one business operation; amend/cancel bookings and manage availability and room reservations through this module. Add a recurring-booking operation with explicit conflict semantics. Avoid callers assembling raw writes across these concepts. | Appointment lifecycle, availability needed for conflict decisions, and room reservations remain in the current database and transaction boundary. The existing four-engineer team remains accountable for changes, releases, data, and incidents. |
| Billing module, current application | Produce nightly billing exports from a narrow, versioned scheduling-owned view or feed. Billing owns export rules and run/checkpoint state if those exist; it must not modify booking state directly. | Scheduling remains authoritative for booked activity; Billing owns any billing-specific state. The same team owns it today. The present read/write paths are not described and require inspection. |
| Local patient-management responsibility | Expose stable patient identifiers and the patient information scheduling needs. Keep appointment lifecycle decisions in Scheduling. | Patient record ownership and invariants are unspecified. Assign their authoritative writes to one local module after inspecting them; do not invent a separate Patient service or assume a shared patient model for billing and scheduling. Current operational ownership stays with the existing team. |

These are responsibility boundaries, not claims that the code already enforces these contracts. Individual stewardship within the team can help maintenance, but no new team or named owner is supplied by the brief. A future extracted service would need explicit responsibility for its contract, releases, migrations, and recovery before extraction.

Recurring appointments are a useful independence test. A change to recurrence conflict handling plausibly touches appointment creation, availability evaluation, and room allocation. In the current topology those changes can share one transaction, one team, and one application release. In the proposed topology the path spans at least three service implementations and their contracts; whether all three must release together depends on interfaces that have not been supplied. Patient and Billing involvement is unknown. There is no change-history evidence with which to claim a measured co-change rate.

Failure behavior also favors keeping Scheduling cohesive. In a remote design, loss of Availability or Room must prevent a booking from being confirmed unless an alternative reservation protocol has already established every required guarantee. Extra dependencies can therefore increase booking failures. The current database remains a common failure dependency, but this proposal supplies no evidence that splitting it improves the clinic journey. By contrast, Billing can be delayed by a day: export failure should be isolated from interactive booking through a scheduled job, bounded resource use, and retry handling within the current application. Measure whether that is insufficient before adding a service.

| Option | Benefit supported by this brief | Dominant cost and decision |
|---|---|---|
| Keep the application with clearer module contracts | Preserves booking atomicity and existing operating capacity; provides a direct place to improve maintainability. | Shares deployments and resources. Choose now; revisit if those become measured constraints. |
| Extract one coarse Scheduling service | Could preserve the booking transaction while isolating a whole capability. | Adds routing, data migration, and operations without an evidenced current isolation need. Defer. |
| Extract Billing alone | Its daily lag tolerance makes an asynchronous contract plausible. | Still adds ownership, deployment, reconciliation, and freshness monitoring. It is the first seam to investigate if a real bottleneck appears, not an extraction justified today. |
| Split all five entities | Smaller codebases are the claimed benefit, not a demonstrated maintenance improvement. | Loses local atomicity, introduces network failure paths and possible remote joins, and leaves all services with one team. Reject now. |

The shared utility package is a question to inspect, not proof of harmful coupling. Stable generic helpers can remain shared. If it contains booking rules, mutable domain models, or utilities that repeatedly force unrelated modules to upgrade together, move that behavior behind the owning module's contract. Check actual imports and changes before deciding. Likewise, inspect billing queries and any patient-related joins before promising data independence.

The smallest next step is a time-boxed design/code-path inspection during recurrence work, with no service extraction: trace booking, a concurrent conflicting booking, cancellation, a recurring request, and a billing export; inspect utility imports and recent changes; record which modules, contracts, and releases must change. This review has not executed that experiment.

Use these observable revisit conditions:

- **Maintainability:** if representative features repeatedly require edits to unrelated module internals, first test a narrower module contract. A proposed pass condition is that recurrence rules can change without changing Billing's public contract or implementation. Failure identifies a coupling to fix; it does not alone justify a network boundary.
- **Independent releases:** record actual releases delayed by unrelated module changes or coordination. Revisit extraction if those delays materially obstruct delivery and a candidate slice can demonstrate a compatible independent release. The team must set an acceptable delay target; none is supplied.
- **Scaling or fault isolation:** measure booking latency, database contention, resource use, and export incidents across representative peak and nightly periods. Revisit Billing separation if its workload causes booking failures or breaches an agreed booking target despite local isolation measures. No such target or measurements are supplied.
- **Billing seam readiness:** before extraction, demonstrate replayable, deduplicated export input and outage recovery within the allowed one-day lag, without blocking bookings or introducing shared writes. These are proposed checks, not results.
- **A finer scheduling split:** require a concrete concurrency/failure protocol that retains the no-overlap requirement during retries, outages, and recurrence changes, plus a measured independent deployment or scaling benefit. Zero confirmed conflicts is a correctness gate; the current split has no supplied evidence that it can meet it.
- **Ownership capacity:** revisit when responsibility and operating capacity can be assigned to the extracted capability, with an actual release or maintenance benefit. Merely creating five services under the same on-call team is not evidence of that benefit.
