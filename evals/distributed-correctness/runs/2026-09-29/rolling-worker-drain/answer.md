`imp-884` failed because rollout cancellations exhausted the queue's failure budget, despite successful checkpoint recovery. The proposed shutdown and retry-limit increase do not establish a graceful drain. Preserve the ordinary failure limit of 3, introduce an explicit safe maintenance handoff using `defer`, and exclude the entire retiring worker generation from new claims before releasing any work.

The proposed `shutdown()` has four material gaps. Setting health unavailable does not affect the `while process_alive` polling loop. `handler_slots=1` does not prove that the pool cannot accumulate additional reserved deliveries. `cancel_all()` leads directly to the broad exception-to-`fail` path, with another exception-to-`fail` conversion in the worker library if an exception escapes. Finally, an 80-second wait followed by `exit(0)` does not prove completion, durable continuation, stopped side effects, or successful queue disposition. Cancellation is checked before a page, so an unbounded `import_page` can also outlast that wait. Evidence: [worker.pseudo](<trial-root>/rolling-worker-drain/project/worker.pseudo:5).

Raising the limit to 10 would postpone the same failure and weaken the requested malformed-input policy. Twice-daily deployments make repeated interruptions an ongoing possibility; a larger budget gives no completion guarantee. Confirmed terminating controls are the queue's failure threshold, lost leases counting as failures, the library's exception mapping, the 80-second pool wait, and the supervisor's 90-second termination grace. The pseudocode's `failure_limit` may configure the queue or may be a separate application setting; its wiring is not supplied. Lease duration, acknowledgment/defer retry semantics, page-operation deadlines, and job-age or progress-stall limits are also unspecified. They must be inventoried before implementation; no broker-specific or Kubernetes-specific limits should be inferred here.

Use bounded resume rather than retaining a worker for the entire 12–24-hour import. Immutable input, independently retained cursors, and supported page replay justify that choice, subject to validating the actual page-replay contract. The required invariants are: unfinished accepted jobs remain discoverable; a checkpoint never skips an uncommitted page; previous effects remain safe on replay; stale owners cannot corrupt progress or a successor's claim; and every job eventually completes or enters an explicit, actionable terminal/escalation state.

The proposed rollout and shutdown protocol is:

1. **Prepare compatible capacity before retirement.** Start sufficient version-42 capacity, with room for old and new workers to overlap. Verify queue access, credentials, checkpoint restoration, supported checkpoint schema, and manifest interpretation. A health response alone is insufficient. Check rollback compatibility too; if older binaries cannot read a new checkpoint format, prevent routing those jobs back to them. If compatible capacity or the timing bounds below cannot be established, hold the rollout before terminating workers.

2. **Close admission across the retiring generation.** Prefer an atomic generation eligibility check at the custom queue's authoritative claim operation. This is a proposed capability, not one shown in the queue contract. The gate must cover A, B, C and any replacement process still belonging to the retiring generation. Record the transition, settle claims racing with it, and inventory all executing and reserved deliveries. Stop local polling, pending receives, prefetch, and task creation as well. If the queue cannot enforce generation eligibility, use a coordinator barrier that stops every retiring consumer and settles all in-flight receives before any delivery is deferred. Retain those controls throughout sequential replacement. A health flag or an application rejection after receiving a delivery does not establish this barrier.

3. **Drain with explicit maintenance state.** Make SIGTERM initiate an idempotent draining transition, rather than ordinary task cancellation. Keep the queue connection, lease renewals, persistence clients, and required credentials alive. Track reserved work as well as the active handler; renewal is only documented while a handler runs, so reserved-delivery lease protection needs verification. Route verified safe maintenance continuations through one disposition owner so neither the handler nor the library accidentally calls `fail` or automatically acknowledges them. Real malformed-input/business errors retain the normal failure path, including when detected during drain.

4. **Use a finite budget inside the 90-second supervisor allowance.** A concrete candidate, measured from SIGTERM receipt, is: stop/settle local admission and inventory by 5 seconds; finish or safely interrupt the bounded page slice by 55 seconds; persist/reconcile the recovery record by 70 seconds; confirm durable handoff and `defer` by 80 seconds; close dependencies and exit by 85 seconds, retaining 5 seconds of margin. The global admission barrier must already be established. These are proposed budgets, not measured capabilities. Bound page calls, persistence, queue requests, and their retries to the remaining time. If a page cannot be safely stopped or completed within its allocation, change slicing/timeouts or explicitly configure a larger finite grace before rolling out. Merely timing out `pool.wait` does not stop writes.

5. **Publish a safe recovery boundary before release.** Commit only the last fully completed contiguous page, tied to the immutable manifest, checkpoint schema/revision, current owner, and ownership epoch. Use conditional writes so a late owner cannot move the cursor backwards, mark a successor's job complete, or clear its claim. Preserve stable `(job_id, page/effect identity)` across all retries. Validate the existing page-reprocessing guarantee; where effects are external, use their actual idempotency/lookup contract or durable reconciliation for an effect whose outcome is unknown. Fencing must be enforced by the protected resource where exclusivity is required; a local lease check alone cannot prevent a paused worker from writing after it resumes.

6. **Defer continuations; acknowledge only completion.** After the handler has stopped producing effects and durable state proves a safe next step, call `defer(delivery)` and confirm its outcome without increasing `failure_count`. This queue already retains the job and cursor, so a new message/outbox system is not automatically necessary. Coordinate release and checkpoint visibility using its actual durable primitives. A successor obtains a new fenced ownership epoch and resumes the same logical job. For a finished import, record durable terminal completion before acknowledgment, and make duplicate delivery after a lost acknowledgment harmless. Neither a checkpoint nor process exit is successful import completion.

7. **Handle deadline and crash cases explicitly.** If a new checkpoint cannot be stored, reuse the last valid durable cursor only when replay is safe and old-owner writes are stopped or fenced; then defer against that known boundary. If recovery or queue-disposition outcome remains uncertain, retain the job/delivery and recovery evidence, do not acknowledge or report success, alert, and pause further retirements. Do not wait indefinitely: the process remains subject to the 90-second kill boundary. Under the supplied contract, a subsequent lost lease still spends a failure. Therefore the drain protocol alone cannot promise budget-free recovery from arbitrary crashes or persistence outages. A stronger maintenance guarantee requires an explicit queue recovery policy that uses durable evidence to classify infrastructure loss separately while retaining ordinary failure limits, finite recovery bounds, and visible unresolved/dead-letter work. Unknown cancellation is not automatically safe maintenance.

8. **Advance rollout only on durable evidence.** Replace further workers only after their predecessor's inventory has reached terminal completion or demonstrably recoverable continuation, and eligible successors have restored released work. Halt on incompatible checkpoints, unknown side effects, repeated lease losses, failed handoffs, or stalled recovery. Continue excluding the retiring generation until it is fully retired. Assign finite total job-age, time-since-progress, and infrastructure-handoff budgets with an explicit escalation owner. The supplied 12–24-hour range is useful input for choosing those thresholds, but is not evidence that any threshold is configured. Maintenance deferrals must not create an endless retry loop.

For `imp-884`, first read its dead-letter record and actual latest checkpoint, reconcile any uncertain effects, and establish that no old owner can write. With a compatible eligible successor and explicit redrive authorization, resume the same `job_id` and manifest from the validated cursor, retaining an audit of the three maintenance failures. Do not reset all failure histories or indiscriminately redrive malformed jobs. No recovery action was taken in this review.
