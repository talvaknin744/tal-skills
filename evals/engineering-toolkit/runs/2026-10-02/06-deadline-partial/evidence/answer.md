**The rollout does not meet the stated timeout or connection lifecycle requirements.** The contract measures the 500 ms useful response target from admission, including acquisition, and reserves a connection for the entire send and its cancellation cleanup ([adapter-contract.md](../final-project/adapter-contract.md)).

- [handler.py](../final-project/handler.py) passes a `time.time()` epoch deadline to `asyncio.timeout_at()`, which uses the event loop’s clock. Acquisition also occurs outside that timeout, and the later `asyncio.wait(..., timeout=0.5)` starts a fresh 500 ms after acquisition. The synthetic [trace](../final-project/trace.jsonl) shows a response at loop time 400.602, **602 ms after admission**.
- Cancelling `child` only requests cancellation. The handler releases `conn-7` while `child_done` is false; another handler acquires it at 400.61, and cleanup finishes at 400.95 ([trace.jsonl](../final-project/trace.jsonl)). Upstream cancellation can reach the same `finally` release while the child is active.

**Bounded correction:** Set one local deadline with `loop.time() + min(0.5, upstream_remaining_budget)`. Apply it to acquisition and the response wait; propagate an earlier upstream cancellation. Register connection ownership with a supervised send before waiting. At expiry, request child cancellation and return `pending` only if dispatch may have occurred. The supervisor retains the connection until the child actually finishes cleanup, then releases it exactly once. Bound the number of connections held in cleanup and reject or backpressure new work when that limit is reached. Reconcile an uncertain send by `shipment_id` and receipt lookup before any resend.

The contract gives no maximum cleanup time or forced-stop guarantee ([adapter-contract.md](../final-project/adapter-contract.md)). The correction can bound caller wait and resource exposure, but **cannot guarantee automatic connection recovery** if cleanup hangs. That guarantee needs an adapter abort or replacement mechanism with a defined completion bound.

**Observable rollout checks:** Measure admission-to-response time, including slow acquisition and an earlier upstream deadline. For an accepted send that times out, verify `cancel_requested`, `child_done`, and `connection_released` occur in that order, and that no second handler acquires the connection before cleanup completes. Stall cleanup to verify the ownership limit causes visible backpressure; then verify capacity returns after cleanup. Finally, verify receipt lookup resolves a pending shipment without a duplicate send. Correlate those events by request, shipment, and connection ID in logs, and monitor response latency, held connections, cleanup age, and unresolved pending shipments. These are proposed checks; I did not execute the synthetic trace or contact a service.
