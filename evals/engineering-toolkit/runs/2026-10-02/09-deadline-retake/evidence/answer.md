**The rollout does not satisfy the timeout or connection lifecycle requirements.**

- [handler.py](../final-project/handler.py) passes a `time.time()` epoch deadline to `asyncio.timeout_at()`, which uses the event loop’s clock. It also starts the separate 500 ms wait *after* connection acquisition, leaving acquisition outside the budget. The synthetic [trace](../final-project/trace.jsonl) shows a response 602 ms after admission, against the [contract’s](../final-project/adapter-contract.md) 500 ms target.
- [handler.py](../final-project/handler.py) requests child cancellation on a pending result but releases the connection without waiting for cleanup. On upstream cancellation, it does not cancel the child at all. In the trace, `conn-7` is released at 400.601 and acquired by another handler at 400.61, while its first send remains active until 400.95. That violates exclusive ownership.
- The carrier accepted the shipment before the timeout. `pending` therefore means **outcome unknown**, and a later resend needs receipt lookup by `shipment_id`.

**Bounded correction:** Set one event-loop deadline at admission, capped by any earlier parent deadline. Apply its remaining budget to acquisition and response waiting, and propagate upstream cancellation. Put acquisition and send under a supervised owner that retains the connection until send cancellation cleanup has *finished*; only then release it. At caller expiry, return a tracked pending outcome if dispatch may have occurred, reconcile the receipt before any resend, and keep the owner accounted for. Cap outstanding owners and unavailable connections, with backpressure when that cap is reached.

The [contract](../final-project/adapter-contract.md) gives no maximum cleanup time or forced-stop guarantee. This correction can bound the caller wait and resource *count*, but cannot guarantee when a stuck connection becomes reusable. A guaranteed recovery time needs an adapter stop/discard contract.

**Proposed observable checks:** Record admission, acquisition, dispatch, cancellation request, child completion, release, receipt reconciliation, and the next successful send under one trace ID. Exercise expiry during acquisition; an earlier upstream cancellation; expiry after carrier acceptance; and cleanup that is slow or never finishes. Verify the caller latency, no send after pre-dispatch expiry, no connection reuse before cleanup, capped outstanding work, no duplicate resend before lookup, and a subsequent useful operation after recovery. These are proposed checks only: the trace is synthetic, and I ran no fixture or external service. Files remain unchanged.
