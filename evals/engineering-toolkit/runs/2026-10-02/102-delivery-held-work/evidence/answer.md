## Review of [queue.md](../final-project/queue.md)

**Broker contract — supplied.** `MessageGroupId` identifies a fair-delivery group, so it should come from the server’s `authenticated_customer`, not the client’s arbitrary `group`. A1 and A2 are both Alpha for delivery and admission accounting, regardless of `g1` and `g2`. The supplied detector considers approximate in-flight and recent processing-time shares. It changes future delivery opportunities; it provides no ordering, per-tenant rate limit, worker reservation, interruption, or completion deadline. Standard delivery may duplicate during visibility. Group identity also grants no data-read authority. [Source](../final-project/queue.md)

**B1 outcome and simultaneous capacity — calculated.** A1 and A2 hold all six permits on `[0,8)`. B1 receives a worker at t=1 but waits seven seconds for a permit, runs for one second, and finishes at t=9: **six seconds after its t=3 useful-finish deadline**. Low broker dwell therefore coexists with zero on-time B1 completions. The draft’s simultaneous promise requires `6 Alpha + 2 quiet = 8` permits from a physical pool of six. Four worker slots do not repair that shortage. Equal received-job counts also miss the cost difference: each Alpha job holds three permits for eight seconds; B1 needs one for one second. [Source](../final-project/queue.md)

**Allocation and waiting — proposed, unexecuted.** The smallest trace-supported correction is a dependency-wide gate, applied *before* starting a job’s parallel operations, with permits charged to the trusted customer: three for an Alpha job and one for B1. Across every worker and other consumer of this six-permit dependency, retain one permit for quiet work and allow Alpha at most five held permits. Because each Alpha job needs three together, only one can start at a time. Count blocked, retry-waiting, prefetched, and settling jobs against the four resident worker slots; keep one slot available to quiet work. Bound local deferred receives to one per customer and 16 seconds as a **proposed** guard, with no dependency operation started while waiting. On expiry, an already accepted job needs a verified durable paused or redelivery path, rather than acknowledgement or loss; pause ownership, lease timing, retention, and a finite retry limit still need design evidence. Arbitrate ready customers by trusted identity with eventual turns for Alpha.

Applied **before t=0** to this fixture, A1 uses three permits, A2 defers, and B1 can use one at t=1 and finish at t=2. The trace’s dependency peak is four, within six; A2 can start after A1 actually releases at t=8 and finish at t=16. This is a conditional result for the supplied schedule, not a demonstrated production deadline or SLO-safe limit. If two simultaneous quiet operations are the intended promise, Alpha can have at most four of six permits; with three-permit Alpha jobs, that still permits only one Alpha job. [Source](../final-project/queue.md)

**Actual release — supplied constraint.** At t=1, extending visibility or signalling cancellation releases **zero** of A1/A2’s six physical permits. They remain held until their operations actually end at t=8. Releasing only a local counter would misstate available capacity; it cannot make the existing B1 finish by t=3. Keep the job and permit charges until operation termination and settlement are observed. Any proposed interruption would also need evidence of recoverable progress and work repeated; neither an interrupt operation nor cancellation completion is supplied. [Source](../final-project/queue.md)
