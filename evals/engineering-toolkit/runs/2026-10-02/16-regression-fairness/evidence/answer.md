## Read-only overload policy for [service.md](../final-project/service.md)

**Protect the database at fleet scope.** Four pods × 40 admitted requests × three concurrent queries can demand **480 connections** from a 100-connection allocation. Admit each synchronous report before query dispatch using a fleet-wide budget of three connection permits per report. Apply the same budget when export workers dispatch jobs. The database pool remains the final physical cap; a per-pod request limit cannot serve as the global limit.

An illustrative starting budget is **90 connections**: 10 held as headroom, six reserved for two B metadata lookups, six for two durable-export workers, and 78 shared across at most 26 other reports. Measure other connection users and query occupancy before setting these values. Keep the B reserve usable end to end: an available worker, routing path, and pool capacity must accompany its permits. A must not borrow it while 500 ms work could delay B beyond 100 ms.

**Bound admission and waiting by tenant and cost.** Use verified tenant identity. Give B metadata a protected, deadline-aware lane; schedule other synchronous work and durable exports fairly across tenants using measured connection time or cost classes. Equal request tokens undercharge exports relative to 5 ms lookups. Bound every transient queue by count, bytes, and wait time, including proxy and pool waits. Recheck the caller’s remaining deadline before dispatch. Reject tenant-quota excess with 429 and temporary shared-capacity excess with 503, with retry guidance; count rejections against the client-visible outcome. B’s 100 ms objective cannot be guaranteed until its burst envelope, latency percentile, and query tail are specified and tested.

**Keep ownership through actual completion.** A synchronous request owns its permits until all issued queries finish or cancellation has *actually* released their connections. A disconnect only detaches the caller. For asynchronous exports, send 202 only after durable persistence and within per-tenant backlog count, byte, and sustainable-drain limits. The persisted job then owns the obligation; a worker takes execution permits at dispatch and records completion and effects before acknowledging it. Retry interrupted jobs with backoff and effect reconciliation. Never delete an accepted export merely because it is old or the service is overloaded.

**Use feedback from useful work.** Track offered, admitted, rejected, timely completed, late, and failed work separately by tenant and class, alongside connection occupancy, pool wait, queue age, accepted-job age, detached queries, and downstream attempts. Exclude 1 ms rejections from admitted-work latency. Start with a measured fixed limit; any adaptive limit must stay below the fleet database budget, decrease on pressure or stalled work, and rise gradually after stable useful completions. Replace immediate threefold 503 retries with bounded, jittered retries that respect remaining deadlines. Autoscaling should consider offered and rejected demand as well as app saturation, while preserving the same global database cap.

**Recovery gate before rollout.** Compare the policy with the current behavior under A’s 1,000/s offer and B’s 10/s offer, mixed lookup/export costs, uneven routing, database slowdown, disconnects, worker interruption after 202, and restored capacity. Verify that connections stay within budget, B meets its defined 100 ms success objective, every accepted export remains accounted for and drains after recovery, queues remain bounded, and admission ramps without a retry surge or controller oscillation. No such recovery test is reported in `service.md`, so these are proposed checks, not observed results.
