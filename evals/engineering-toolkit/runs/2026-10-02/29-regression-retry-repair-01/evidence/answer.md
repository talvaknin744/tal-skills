The incident describes **up to nine Inventory attempts per client request**: three Gateway attempts × three Checkout attempts. At 200 client requests per second, that is **1,800 Inventory attempts per second**, a **9× amplification** and 1,600 extra attempts per second over a single call. This estimate assumes every attempt times out and excludes client retries. If all nine one-second waits occur serially, the request takes roughly nine seconds plus overhead; Gateway timeout and queue behavior are unspecified, so actual latency and overlap cannot be pinned down. [Incident evidence](../final-project/incident.md)

The main propagation risks are the immediate retries into an already slow Inventory service, growing active requests and queues, and work continuing after its caller gives up. Missing end-to-end deadline propagation lets each layer spend time beyond the product’s two-second preference. A caller timeout alone does not show that the underlying Inventory work has stopped or that its connection and capacity are reusable.

**Bounded correction:** For this read-only availability check, disable timeout retries at both layers initially, set a two-second deadline from Gateway receipt, and propagate the remaining budget through Checkout. Keep the Inventory attempt capped at one second, including its resource acquisition wait. Cap concurrent Inventory work at a level established from measured capacity; reject immediately with temporary unavailability when that cap is full. Retain a capacity slot until the underlying work finishes or its resource is safely disposed of. Successful calls resume through the same limit as Inventory recovers.

Validate under a controlled slow-Inventory test at 200 client requests per second: dispatched Inventory attempts stay at or below 200 per second, Gateway returns a clear unavailable response within two seconds, active Inventory work stays within the configured cap, and queue depth does not grow. Check cancellation and resource release after callers leave, then restore Inventory and confirm useful requests succeed without a retry surge. **These are proposed checks, not observed results**; the supplied project contains incident evidence only.
