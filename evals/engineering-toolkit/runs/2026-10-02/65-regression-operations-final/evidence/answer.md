The worst case in [incident.md](../final-project/incident.md) is **3 Gateway attempts × 3 Checkout attempts = 9 Inventory calls per client request**. At 200 client requests per second, that is up to **1,800 Inventory calls per second** after the retries ramp up: 1,600 more than the 200 calls needed without retries. With each call occupying one second, roughly 1,800 Inventory calls could be active at once if capacity permits. Immediate retries concentrate that load on an already slow service.

The main propagation risks are the multiplied load, growing worker and connection occupancy, and queued work continuing after its caller has given up. That can spread the outage into Checkout and Gateway, including traffic unrelated to Inventory. Nine seconds is an illustrative serial wait for nine one-second attempts, **not a measured response time**: Gateway’s per-attempt timeout is unspecified, so attempts may overlap. Client retries could increase load further and are outside the 200 requests per second estimate.

**Bounded correction:** For this read-only availability check, disable timeout retries at both Gateway and Checkout. Start a **1.8-second end-to-end deadline** when Gateway receives the request and pass the *remaining* allowance to Checkout; allow one Inventory call, with at most 100 ms for queue or connection acquisition and 1 second for the call. Return a clear temporary-unavailability response when that budget expires. Cap Inventory-bound concurrency at a value supported by measured capacity, keep its queue finite, and reject excess work promptly. Cancellation should stop work where supported; a connection or concurrency permit must remain accounted for until that work has actually stopped or its resource has been safely disposed.

**Validation before rollout:** Under a controlled 200-request-per-second slow-Inventory test, verify at most one dispatched Inventory call per client request, responses within two seconds, bounded queue depth and active work, and no continuing growth after callers time out. Then restore Inventory and verify useful requests succeed again without a retry surge. Correlate Gateway outcomes with Checkout and Inventory attempts to distinguish user impact from component timeouts.

**Completion record:** Currently, Inventory waits one second per attempt; Gateway has no overall deadline, Checkout receives none, and neither layer cancels abandoned work. The proposed caller allowance is 1.8 seconds, including acquisition and response time. The incident does not identify the runtime’s cancellation or join primitives, cleanup duration, or deadline transport. Those must be checked in the implementation: cap any caller-side cleanup wait by the remaining deadline, retain ownership of unfinished work, and reuse resources only after observed completion or safe disposal. Verify that deadline handoff cannot renew the allowance through transit time or clock differences. These are proposed checks; no production behavior was changed or tested.
