## Smallest supported correction

Gate **execution before starting dependency operations**. Account by authenticated customer across the shared worker and dependency scope: one worker slot for a job throughout execution, blocking, retry wait, and settlement; one dependency permit per operation until that operation actually terminates. Reserve an Alpha job’s three permits before dispatch so it does not occupy a worker while waiting for its remaining permits. Count retries and duplicate deliveries against the same physical budgets.

For the *fixed B1 trace*, keeping one worker slot and one dependency permit usable for the protected work would let B1 run from t=1 to t=2 **if it is delivered at t=1**. With that reserve, A1 and A2 cannot both start their three-permit fan-outs at t=0; one must be deferred. This is a feasibility calculation, not a production deadline guarantee or an SLO-safe limit. A two-permit quiet reserve instead leaves at most four permits for Alpha, still allowing only one whole Alpha job at a time. The final reserve and admission limits need evidence about demand, other consumers, and delivery timing.

Keep deferred, already accepted jobs recoverable and eligible for eventual arbitration; bound local prefetch, wait age, and repeat delivery, and specify overflow and expiry dispositions. Acknowledge only after the accepted job’s completion or an explicit terminal disposition. Use job identity to reconcile duplicate deliveries and effects.

Before adopting a limit, compare broker dwell with **useful on-time completions**, permit and worker occupancy, deferred age, and Alpha’s eventual progress over the same window. Exercise split client groups, costly Alpha work beside Beta, a stop signal before actual operation termination, duplicate delivery, and restart after acceptance. Confirm that permits remain charged until release and that recovery drains accepted work without a redelivery surge.
