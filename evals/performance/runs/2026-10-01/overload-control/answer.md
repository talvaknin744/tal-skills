# Read-only overload-control design for the shared report service

This policy protects B's useful metadata completions within its 100 ms objective, prevents database connection exhaustion, gives tenants a bounded share of resources, and preserves every durably accepted export. It is a proposed design, not an implemented or tested result. The only project evidence is `project/service.md`; no workload, query timings, deployment configuration, or persistence implementation is available.

## Evidence and acceptance boundaries

The present configuration can admit `4 pods × 40 reports × 3 concurrent queries = 480` simultaneous database operations against a service allocation of 100 connections. Unlimited FIFO waiting hides the excess in memory and makes fresh B metadata wait behind A's large exports. Equal request-count tokens price a 5 ms lookup and a 500 ms export equally, despite their very different occupancy. A per-pod cap neither establishes a fleet database cap nor proves fairness. At scale-out it multiplies again.

Useful synchronous success means the promised report arrives before its useful deadline. For B metadata, use a 100 ms end-to-end budget, including authentication, waiting, execution, and response work; the required percentile and client/network measurement boundary still need agreement. A rejection, failed execution, late success, and successful on-time response are separate outcomes. Classify metadata and export work from the authenticated route and validated work shape, not a caller-supplied priority.

Synchronous admission is a transient execution promise. Durable export acceptance occurs at the persistence commit, before HTTP 202. Disconnect before that commit cancels unaccepted intake where possible; an uncertain commit must be reconciled. After commit, the server owns the accepted job even if the 202 response is lost. Rejection must occur before effects or persistence; after acceptance, overload cannot revoke the eventual-completion contract.

## Admission placement and resource budgets

Start with fixed limits. Use a fleet-wide budget of **90 service database connections**, with the remaining 10 of the allocation held as a safety margin. These are provisional operating limits, not measured optimal values. Include synchronous reports, asynchronous workers, persistence, retries, authentication queries, maintenance, and idle pooled connections. Reduce the usable budget if an inventory reveals additional consumers. Enforce a database-side service cap of 100 as the final boundary.

Use these initial allocations inside the 90-connection budget:

| Path or resource | Budget and enforcement scope | Decision and disposition | Lifecycle owner |
|---|---|---|---|
| All service database access | 90 total open connections fleet-wide; every connection consumer participates | No connection is opened or checked out beyond its allocation; allocation failure rejects unaccepted work with 503 | Pool/connection allocator owns open connections until they are actually closed; executor owns checked-out use until settlement |
| B metadata | 12 dedicated connections fleet-wide, equivalent to four three-query reports; additional common capacity may be borrowed | Admit only with three available units and enough remaining deadline; reject unavailable admission with 503 | Report execution task owns all three units until every issued query settles |
| Durable export execution | 12 guaranteed connections, equivalent to four jobs; borrow common capacity up to a total of 30 connections/10 jobs | Fair dispatcher runs persisted jobs only with three units; unavailable capacity leaves jobs durably pending | Durable dispatcher and execution task; capacity transfers with actual execution, not the HTTP caller |
| Common report capacity | 60 connections/20 report slots shared among authenticated tenant/class queues | Tenant-fair scheduling and occupancy credits; quota rejection is 429, temporary resource rejection is 503 | Scheduler owns a waiter until dispatch/cancel; execution task owns permits after dispatch |
| Persistence and control work | Six reserved connections fleet-wide; at most four concurrent export persistence commits, leaving two for required control/auth work | Intake requires backlog/storage credit before commit; insufficient credit rejects before persistence; control work remains bounded | Commit task owns its connection and intake reservation through the actual commit outcome |

The guaranteed allocations sum to 90: `12 + 12 + 60 + 6`. Do not lend B's dedicated capacity to 500 ms exports, because non-preemptible borrowed work could delay B beyond 100 ms. Keep the persistence/control reserve unavailable to bulk work. Durable work may borrow the common allocation, but borrowed capacity is reclaimed by stopping further dispatches; never release permits for still-running work to manufacture capacity. B and durable guarantees refer to healthy usable capacity, not completion guarantees during a database outage.

Put a cheap bounded ingress guard before authentication/body buffering, then tenant/class admission after verified authentication and before expensive parsing, report construction, pool waiting, or the three-query fan-out. Durable intake also needs its backlog reservation before persistence. The guard must bound unauthenticated CPU, body bytes, sockets, and logging cost; those numerical limits cannot be derived from `service.md` and are a required measurement before deployment. Reserve enough ingress/auth capacity for B's arrival rate and deadline. If authentication uses the database, it must use the bounded control allocation and be tested as part of B's path.

Acquire the report's three connection units atomically, then dispatch its three leaves. Do not admit a request that independently queues three pool acquisitions. Use one consistent order: intake/tenant admission, execution allocation, then connection checkout. On any partial acquisition, release only resources that have not begun work. Database retries must remain within the same connection, attempt, and deadline budgets; an extra leaf requires an extra permit before dispatch.

The pool configuration must also respect the fleet cap; an active-query limiter alone does not limit idle open connections. At the current four-pod topology, allocations such as 24/24/21/21 sum to 90. Synchronous and asynchronous work in a pod share that allocation. New pods receive portions of the same 90, not another fixed 40 or 90. Reallocate only after old pooled connections are drained/closed. Route centrally scheduled work to a pod with the required allocated capacity, including protected B capacity, so sticky routing cannot strand B's guarantee. A limiter outage fails closed for new common work; existing execution retains ownership and accepted jobs stay pending. No expiring distributed lease may free capacity while its prior owner could still be querying. Recovery requires proving the former owner/connection has stopped before reuse.

## Tenant budgets, waiting, and accepted work

Account in **connection-milliseconds**, as well as enforcing hard connection counts. If all three queries hold a connection for the full listed report duration, the initial estimates are 15 connection-ms for metadata and 1,500 for an export: a 100-fold cost difference. Measure actual per-leaf hold times and reconcile estimates against actual occupancy. These calculations are conditional estimates, not measurements.

A conservative initial refill rate is 80% of each allocation's connection-time capacity. B's protected lane then has 9,600 connection-ms/s; its stated 10 metadata requests/s would consume approximately 150 connection-ms/s under the assumption above. The common lane has 48,000 connection-ms/s, split equally between A and B while both demand it; unused shares can be borrowed. Limit common bursts to one second of credits and retain the hard concurrency cap. Durable execution has at least 9,600 connection-ms/s, split equally between the two tenants when both have eligible accepted jobs. Debit bulk, synchronous, and retry occupancy to the originating tenant; disconnect does not erase its accounting. Cost-estimation errors must not block accepted jobs forever: the durable minimum service share remains eligible while new intake is throttled.

Use deficit round-robin or an equivalent work-conserving scheduler with bounded cost credits across authenticated tenant/class queues. B metadata gets its dedicated lane. Each tenant has a positive share of the common lane, and accepted jobs have a separate positive service share; large A jobs cannot consume those reservations. Within a tenant's durable queue, choose oldest eligible jobs, skipping only jobs in a persisted retry-delay state. Fairness is fleet-wide, with no independent per-pod tenant buckets that can be multiplied or defeated by uneven routing. Tenant identities are already verified; restrict limiter keys to the service's registered tenants and keep metric labels bounded rather than labeling every job or request.

Replace unlimited FIFO waiting with the following provisional bounds:

- B metadata: at most eight waiting requests and 512 KiB of retained request state fleet-wide; maximum queue wait 20 ms. Recheck the remaining 100 ms budget after waiting and immediately before dispatch. Size the execution safety margin from measured tails; the stated 5 ms duration alone does not establish it.
- Common synchronous work: at most 32 waiters and 2 MiB retained state fleet-wide, no more than 16 waiters per tenant. Maximum wait is 25 ms for metadata and 100 ms for exports, or the remaining caller deadline minus the measured execution margin, whichever is smaller. Reject overflow before fan-out with 503; remove abandoned waiters immediately. Expired queued work never reaches the database.
- Executor and pool waiting: no extra executor queue and no unbudgeted pool wait after dispatch. Reserve capacity before starting. Inventory and bound proxy/socket queues too; their existing count/byte/age limits are missing from the evidence.
- Durable backlog: initial fleet intake ceiling of 480 outstanding jobs and 128 MiB of persisted payload, including pending, running, and retry-wait jobs. Each tenant initially gets 240 jobs/64 MiB and can borrow unused capacity only within the fleet ceiling. Reject a new payload over the proposed 1 MiB maximum before persistence; validate that limit against the real API contract. Stop accepting new jobs when oldest pending age exceeds 60 seconds. Count, bytes, and age all participate in the intake decision.
- Durable prefetch: claim no more jobs than available execution slots, with at most 10 running jobs and 10 MiB of loaded payload fleet-wide. Do not build another worker-local backlog or immediately requeue failed jobs in a loop. Persist bounded exponential retry delays with jitter.

The byte/count/age numbers above are proposed safety settings requiring payload and storage measurements. At the guaranteed four export slots and an assumed 500 ms per job, 480 jobs represent approximately 60 seconds of healthy drain work. Failures or slower effects invalidate that estimate. **The age ceiling closes new intake; it never deletes or expires an accepted job.** Existing jobs older than the ceiling remain owned, execute fairly, and raise an operational alert. Lowering limits below an existing backlog also closes intake without discarding it.

Commit a durable registry record containing job identity, trusted tenant, payload location/size, state, retry eligibility, ownership, and effect/reconciliation identity before returning 202. The intake reservation becomes a persisted outstanding-job accounting entry at commit. On restart, rebuild counters from this registry rather than resetting them to zero. Workers transition accounted jobs through pending, running, retry-wait, and complete; acknowledge completion only after the documented effects are durably established. Interruption after an effect but before acknowledgement uses the existing reconciliation contract before retrying. That contract is absent from `service.md`, so safe handoff around this boundary must be verified before implementation. There is no business-approved terminal discard in the supplied evidence: do not substitute deletion, TTL expiry, or an unmonitored dead-letter queue for eventual completion.

Caller disconnect cancels a queued transient waiter and releases its waiting state. Once a query has been issued, a separate execution owner observes cancellation/settlement and retains the database and admission permits until all issued operations actually stop or finish. A response deadline may detach the caller without stopping execution. Release exactly once after final settlement, track detached outstanding work and failures, and continue charging its occupancy. A persisted export's ownership never belongs to the disconnected HTTP request.

## Feedback, retries, and recovery

Remove immediate retry amplification. Current callers can make four attempts per logical request, potentially turning 1,000 A arrivals/s into 4,000 attempts/s. Use one retry owner, at most one additional attempt, a finite overall deadline, and jittered backoff that respects Retry-After. An overloaded server returns 503 with a stable reason and a Retry-After hint; tenant quota enforcement returns 429. B generally has no retry opportunity when its remaining 100 ms budget cannot cover backoff plus useful execution. Do not retry another pod simply because it shares the same saturated database. Confirm clients actually honor the new policy. Lost responses after durable persistence need the existing identity/reconciliation contract; transport retries do not imply that no job was accepted.

Define a separate application deadline-exceeded disposition. Its exact HTTP mapping needs the missing API contract; do not label deadline expiry as tenant quota exhaustion. Stale/degraded report results are not an authorized substitute for the promised result, so reject unavailable new work instead of inventing a cache fallback.

First validate the fixed-budget policy. Only then enable adaptive concurrency over the common/borrowable capacity, leaving protected reservations intact when physical capacity supports them. Use one-second windows and separate admitted metadata, admitted export, and durable-job populations. Record queue wait, execution-to-final-query-settlement latency, total useful-response latency, connection occupancy, oldest live operation, pool wait, useful completions, late completions, errors, rejection reason, and offered logical demand/attempts. The 1 ms rejection response population never enters the execution latency baseline or useful-completion histogram.

A proposed controller reduces common capacity by 25% after three consecutive windows with over 90% occupancy plus execution latency above twice the measured per-class baseline, or B queue age above 20 ms. A connection-cap failure or stale/no completion samples while outstanding operations exceed their expected duration immediately stops new common dispatch and prevents increases. Maintain an upper bound of 90 total service connections, including any downward usable-capacity change. Reducing a limit stops admission; it does not revoke live permits. Keep 24 connection units for B and durable reservations only while that much physical capacity is usable, plus necessary control capacity. If capacity falls below the promised reservations, proportionally reduce allocations within the actual total, explicitly report objective risk, and reject excess unaccepted work. Do not claim a latency guarantee through a stalled database.

Signal flow is: offered tenant demand → bounded ingress → tenant/class admission → fair queues → execution/connection gates → database; admitted queue/execution/occupancy signals drive the limiter. Offered demand, rejection, resource utilization, backlog age, and ready/warm capacity drive capacity decisions. Load balancing uses available allocated capacity and admitted useful-work health, not a median improved by fast rejections. Autoscaling must not treat fast failures or fewer completed requests as reduced demand. Adding pods redistributes the fixed database budget and cannot increase the database's usable capacity by itself.

Recovery uses actual usable capacity and successful useful work:

1. Preserve the registry and live permit ownership. Verify database health, connection counts, warmed pods, and actual stop/settlement of old detached work. Stagger connection opening and worker claims after restart.
2. Run jittered metadata probes at up to one per second fleet-wide within protected capacity; retain the durable minimum service share when effects can safely progress. Low/no traffic or missing signals cannot justify an increase. Probes must respect the same connection/deadline limits.
3. Reopen common dispatch only after five consecutive one-second windows with successful database probes, B metadata total p95 below 80 ms, occupancy below 70%, B queue age below 10 ms, and no operation beyond its verified execution bound. These are provisional thresholds to validate. A controller state change alone is not recovery.
4. Restore common capacity by at most three connection units every five seconds, with jitter; recheck all conditions and decrease/close on regression. Drain accepted jobs within the 30-connection async ceiling and tenant shares. Stop intake while backlog count, bytes, or age remains over its threshold. Cold caches and expensive retry-wait jobs still consume their real budget.
5. Provide an operational switch back to the validated fixed allocation, with no cap increase above physical capacity. If the coordinator or telemetry fails, freeze increases, fail closed for new common admissions, retain bounded protected execution where ownership is known, and keep accepted work durably pending.

## Unexecuted validation procedure and acceptance conditions

No load, cancellation, crash, or recovery test has run. Use the same workload mix, database allocation, logical arrival sequence, client policy, and duration for the current baseline and the proposed fixed policy. Record attempts separately when retry policies differ. Then compare adaptive control against the validated fixed policy.

- Drive A at 1,000 reports/s and B at 10 metadata reports/s, first with 5 ms metadata, then with 500 ms A exports, then a mixed and bursty population. Capture offered/admitted/rejected work, connection-time by tenant/class, client-visible useful throughput, B deadline success and latency, queue count/bytes/age, total open/active connections, and downstream attempts. The current baseline is analytically unsafe; its actual throughput/latency has not been measured.
- Increase database latency fivefold, then stall issued queries. Verify the hard 90-connection operating bound and 100-connection allocation, bounded waiting, explicit rejection, and no capacity release on an HTTP timeout/disconnect. Inject cancellation before acquisition, in queues, after one/two/all three leaves start, and racing completion. Fresh work must be admitted after actual cleanup, with no leaked or double-released permits.
- Put expensive A traffic beside B metadata and both tenants' durable jobs. Validate B's 100 ms objective at the agreed percentile under healthy protected capacity; verify its reserved slots are never occupied by bulk work. Validate positive durable and common tenant shares with no starvation. If heavy queries degrade B even with reserved connections, database CPU/I/O isolation or stricter bulk limits are required: connection fairness alone is insufficient.
- Exercise sticky/uneven routing, four-to-eight-pod scale-out, pod restart/loss, coordinator failure, stale telemetry, low traffic, and simultaneous recovery probes. Fleet pool allocations and all known bypass paths must remain inside the same budget; sample absence and fast rejection must never cause a limit increase.
- Interrupt durable jobs immediately after persistence, before response, during execution, after documented effects, and before acknowledgement. Every accepted job must remain accounted for with recoverable ownership and effects consistent with its existing contract. Overflow rejects before acceptance; old accepted jobs are retained. Restart must not reset backlog counters or create a redelivery surge.
- Recover the database and introduce cold pods/caches. Measure time until stable useful service: sustained B objective success, backlog age decreasing under fair bounded drain, no permit/accounting leaks, bounded attempts, and no oscillation or renewed connection exhaustion for at least ten minutes after the recovery ramp.

Before deployment, obtain actual connection-hold and tail timings, maximum payload/retained-state sizes, proxy/auth costs, all database consumers, maximum topology, objective percentile, HTTP deadline semantics, and durable effect/acknowledgement reconciliation details. Validate the provisional numeric settings against those facts. This design supplies a concrete bounded starting policy and test criteria; it supplies no executed performance proof.
