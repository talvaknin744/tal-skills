# Vercel cached body recovery, continuation r01

This continuation recovers complete published prose from 659 cached article responses that the original 120 word minimum rejected. It also identifies 222 archive navigation responses in that old gap set: 46 blog pages and 176 changelog pages. Seven responses retain partial prose because embedded video remains part of their article content. Four historical post URLs still redirect to docs, pricing or the blog index, and their cached feed entries contain only excerpts.

The old crawl, reports and sealed archive remain unchanged. New extraction fragments, text, source hash bindings, parser candidates, failed attempts and checks live in a separate external continuation directory. No network requests were made.

The recovery rule requires a matching final canonical URL, a specific headline, one article element, one publisher marked blog body container and paragraph or list prose. Every recovered body has that publisher marker. Its source response, complete cached article HTML and recovered text have independent hashes. The recovered prose ranges from 22 to 119 words. The original cutoff was therefore a content length limitation, rather than evidence that these articles had no accessible body. A [short dependency caching notice](https://vercel.com/changelog/yarn-2-dependency-caching-now-supported) and an [October 2019 update](https://vercel.com/blog/changelog-october-2019) provide concrete retained reproduction cases.

The navigation finding uses publication identity, rather than a smaller text threshold. The cached [blog archive](https://vercel.com/blog/page/10) and [changelog archive](https://vercel.com/changelog/page/10) identify themselves as CollectionPage documents and contain article cards. Card summaries are not the bodies of those linked articles. The proposed article denominator is 2,026 after these 222 navigation records are separated from the original 2,248 records. This is a new classification proposal, not a rewrite of the historical inventory.

Three short release articles ended with a promotional card linking to a different publication. Inspecting the DOM established that its Read more label belonged to that card. Removing the card from the recovered prose preserves the complete original HTML separately and resolves those three initial review cases. Media handling remains conservative: six moved customer stories and one changelog article contain video. Their visible prose and media references were archived, but those responses are not marked fully recovered. Image references within the 659 recovered prose bodies are retained without claiming image interpretation or asset retrieval.

The external parser passed 14 focused checks. These include the known short article failure, a historical update, both archive types, docs and pricing redirects, canonical identity mismatch, missing publisher body markers, image only content, video content, ancillary card removal and source hash bindings for every recovery. The initial newly authored suite passed 13 checks and failed one assertion because an inline link introduced whitespace into a sentence. The full sentence assertion was corrected to normalize whitespace. Its previous source and failed log remain preserved.

The parser remains external. Integrating this recovery rule requires the root task's review of its narrow publisher contract and retained cases. Automatic body extraction is separate from substantive reading, and these results do not establish deleted, private or unlisted publisher history, complete media coverage or current product behavior.
