# Grafana Labs English blog refresh

Research date: 14 October 2026. The retained scope is English `/blog/{article}` routes, excluding documentation, translations, tags, categories and archive directories. Official sitemap, feed and uncapped public archive traversal identify 2033 URLs, adding four to the retained 2029 URL baseline. Archive page 102 exposes 13 metadata rows and no next page link. All 2033 URLs received successful HTTP responses. Of these, 2018 article bodies passed automatic extraction, one short survey post remained below its substantive body threshold and 14 routes redirected outside the blog collection. Final crawl counters and two deliberate reading scopes appear in [the receipt](grafana-labs.json).

Initial failed requests caused by a collector canonical redirect defect remain preserved. The corrected collector retried the complete discovered frontier. Local reclassification applied the final article URL boundary to all preserved responses. Fourteen historical routes now redirect to product, security advisories or event pages; those responses do not recover their original article bodies and remain explicit gaps. A publisher sitemap and exhausted pagination establish the available declared collection, not lifetime coverage of unlisted, deleted or private posts. Automatic extraction does not mean every article was read.

## Useful additions and limits

The cardinality account shows that metric series are also stored and emitted by the application. Unbounded identifier labels consume emitter memory and scrape CPU as well as backend resources. A separate transaction identifier series at constant value can make `increase` report zero despite real transactions. A deferred `microservice-operations` extension could require bounded metric dimensions and compare observed emitted counts with an independent request ledger under churn. Keep useful bounded dimensions, and verify actual backend and instrumentation library limits rather than banning all labels. [Complete cardinality article](https://grafana.com/blog/what-is-high-cardinality-and-is-it-as-scary-as-people-make-it-out-to-be-/)

The native profiling account compares workloads with profiling enabled and disabled, identifies symbol and architecture requirements, and separates CPU profiles from possible off CPU observations. A deferred `performance-diagnosis` extension could require symbol coverage and observer overhead in any claimed native profile comparison. The reported workload overhead and sampling rate are examples, not a general bound. Verify current kernel, architecture, profiler signal maturity and build symbols before an implementation. This research executed no profiler or production benchmark. [Complete native profiling article](https://grafana.com/blog/continuous-profiling-for-native-code-understanding-the-what-why-and-how/)
