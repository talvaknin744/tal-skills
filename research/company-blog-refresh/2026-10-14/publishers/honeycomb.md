## Useful additions and limits

The current adaptive sampling article supplies a useful processor boundary: its first matching rule records an OpenTelemetry probability threshold, while ordinary tail sampler policies use a different decision contract. Decisions are per Collector instance, so every span of a trace needs the same instance. Trace count and span count bounds do not establish a byte bound, and the throughput goal applies per instance. An implementation must verify the installed processor's actual decision contract and estimator support. The candidate's new `performance-diagnosis` trace guidance can support that distinction without prescribing this beta processor. No benchmark or Collector deployment ran here. [Complete adaptive sampling article](https://www.honeycomb.io/blog/how-adaptive-tail-sampling-works)

A deferred diagnostic addition concerns the observer's own load. Head sampling discarded rare slow collect loop traces, but asking the unhealthy sampler to tail sample its own traces risked increasing that load. A separate temporary sampling cluster supplied bounded slow trace capture. The discriminating case asks for diagnostics on an overloaded Collector and passes only when capture overhead, useful evidence and shutdown bounds remain explicit. The article's particular batch limit is not a default for another workload. [Complete diagnostic account](https://www.honeycomb.io/blog/tracing-refinery)

The historical sampling guide distinguishes trace consistent decisions from independent span dropping and explains the buffering cost of tail decisions. Read its instrumentation cost, decision types and missing span troubleshooting sections. Its product maturity statements are historical and require current documentation before implementation. [Sampling guide](https://www.honeycomb.io/blog/getting-at-the-good-stuff-how-to-sample-traces-in-honeycomb)
