# Identity, duplicates, and corrections

Define identity from the source contract. Delivery identity, logical event identity, entity key, and revision can have different scopes. Specify whether repeated identity means identical immutable payload, a newer complete replacement, or a required delta. A same-identity payload conflict needs visible reconciliation or rejection; a silent first/last-arrival choice invents business authority.

Compose admission and contribution deliberately. A duplicate arrival or replay of the same accepted version contributes once. Under an authoritative complete-replacement contract, a newer revision replaces the old contribution; for a sum, retract the old amount before adding the new one. An old revision cannot undo it. Required deltas instead need their own identity and gap/order protocol; a generic “keep latest” query loses information.

For a correction that moves a record to another key or window, arbitrate its revision and evaluate admission for both the old contribution's removal and the replacement's addition before changing either. Apply the move as one correction disposition only when both changes are permitted, with a recoverable state/output boundary that prevents a partial move. If either change would alter an immutable finalized result, preserve both current states and the accepted revision, and reject the whole correction with an audit reason. An independently authorized compensation contract may represent a correction separately while leaving finalized records unchanged. Handle deletion as a change with retained authority, so old replay cannot resurrect removed state. Sink keys and update/retraction support must preserve the selected result.

Check a contribution of 10 in window A, then finalize A while window B remains open. Deliver a newer revision moving that contribution to B with amount 12. Under immutable-finality policy, A stays 10, B gains nothing, and the accepted revision stays unchanged with a rejected-correction audit. Repeat with B finalized and A open: the rejected move also leaves A's 10 intact. Replay the correction after restart and require the same disposition.

Retain identity, revision, and prior-contribution evidence for as long as a repeat can still be admitted or recovery can rebuild a changed result. Align that horizon with source retention, operator state, correction policy, and checkpoint/bootstrap paths. Expiring a deduplication entry can admit a repeat again. A proven permanently closed window may allow retirement earlier than source history, provided all old deliveries are rejected independently and the required audit remains meaningful.

Check one original, identical repeat, newer replacement, old-version replay, and same-version conflicting payload in different arrival orders. Assert sum/count or joined values independently, then repeat at state-expiry and recovery boundaries. Query-level deduplication does not demonstrate broker delivery or external-effect atomicity; see [sources.md](sources.md) for the narrower engine semantics.
