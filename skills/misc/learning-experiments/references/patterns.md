# Experiment patterns

## Reading without usable knowledge

State what the text must enable: an explanation, decision, proof, procedure or reliable lookup. Preview the main structure briefly and write one question. At a meaningful boundary, close the source and explain a relationship or attempt an application; compare with the source and repair. Track deferred details so a broad overview does not become incomplete coverage.

If the obstacle is terminology or a prerequisite, supply targeted instruction. If it is a misleading analogy, identify its predicted consequence and check a boundary case. Leave a precise question and page/section as a restart cue. No fixed sentence-by-sentence interruption timer is needed.

## Notes and mind maps

Ask the learner to propose a small organization, then compare alternatives and inspect a questionable relationship. Label implication, equivalence, contrast, prerequisite and analogy accurately. Preserve exact definitions, conditions, quantifiers and required proof steps in accessible source notes. Use a table or prose if it represents the reasoning better.

Judge the representation by closed-source reconstruction and fresh application, not visual density, word count or aesthetics. AI may retrieve candidate terms or critique an attempt; learner-generated comparison remains the assessment when that is the target. Expert examples can still provide instruction.

## Repeated errors or overconfidence

If a study routine is unclear, trace one topic from first exposure through its assessment: action, purpose, time and observed result. Choose one questionable step for the experiment. A brief premortem can generate a testable concern; it is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis from confidence alone.

Separate conceptual method choice from procedural execution. Locate the first consequential error, practise that component with feedback, then reconnect it to a complete fresh task. Ask for confidence before feedback only when it helps calibration; compare it with verified correctness. Treat repeated success on familiar prompts as narrower evidence than fresh delayed success.

Vary a relevant condition or representation at comparable difficulty before adding complexity. Repeated failure calls for a prerequisite or instruction check. Familiar fluency can be useful; unnecessary struggle is not the goal.

## Starting, attention and resumption

Observe the actual barrier. Prepare one permitted problem, source and workspace; choose a small first action and a real stopping point. Use an if/then cue tied to the interruption: “If I reach for a solution, I first write where my attempt stopped.” Remove one observed distraction. Keep a visible restart question after an interruption.

When the barrier is inadequate time, illness or substantial fatigue, revise the workload rather than reclassifying it as laziness. A brief fallback is partial work, not completion of the original appointment. Recovery can be chosen for the user's own goals; avoid turning every break into another compulsory optimization exercise.

## Daily-life transfer

Choose a reversible real task, state the decision or output the new knowledge should improve, and test it with competent feedback. Keep uncertain causal explanations separate from observations. General skill transfer is a question to test, not an automatic benefit of a new study technique.

Hebrew prompts:

- **מה ניסיתי, מה קרה בפועל, ומה אני רק משער לגבי הסיבה?**
- **איזה שינוי אחד אנסה, מה הוא מחליף בזמן שלי, ובאיזו משימה חדשה אבדוק אותו?**
- **מה השאלה המדויקת שאשאיר לעצמי כדי לחזור למשימה בקלות?**
