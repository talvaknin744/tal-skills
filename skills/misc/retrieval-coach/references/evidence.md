# Evidence boundaries

- [Karpicke and Blunt, 2011](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_Science.pdf) supports retrieval for later comprehension/inference in its tested science-text conditions; it does not establish universal superiority of one quiz format.
- [Cepeda et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/) relates useful spacing to the retention horizon. The workflow's starter dates are operational heuristics.
- [Sung's Anki discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eauQac_23R0) motivates repairing repeated failures and varying successful cards. His numerical card limits and repetition thresholds are coaching suggestions.
- [Sung's skill-development discussion](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=O46wlbRsAK4&t=895s) distinguishes varied contexts from harder tasks. The skill applies this distinction without adopting promised speed gains.
- [Sung's memory ladder](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ldaCYNWUW5M&t=1138s) includes recurring use and retrieval; observing exposure alone is not evidence of successful recall.
- [Sung's mathematics Q&A](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mQtSv7ZbbIw&t=5194s) distinguishes method selection from execution; compare timed and untimed attempts before attributing an error to fluency.

These are sources for components, not validation of this tutoring system. Available transcript reading does not verify audio, diagrams or every linked source. Score observed work, not a claimed learner type, permanent memory, effort or confidence.
