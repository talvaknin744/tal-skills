# Evidence boundaries

This workflow adapts a partial-channel Justin Sung transcript review and prior learning-science research. It is an original planning design, not a validated package or a promise of a grade.

- [Sung: retrieval priority](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q6DnYfLQFCI&t=1880s) and [short exam runway](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q6DnYfLQFCI&t=3907s): coaching advice to stabilize retrieval while developing encoding gradually.
- [Sung: external calibration](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m8AcAxOqUBo&t=560s): compare preparation with actual assessment standards.
- [Sung: outcomes versus proxies](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2crhrbqCLzU&t=1818s): useful planning distinction; claimed productivity multipliers are not adopted.
- [Sung: total time to competence](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=goHaIfMcfWo&t=3288s): include later relearning, correction and verification rather than measuring first-pass speed alone.
- [Karpicke and Blunt, 2011](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_Science.pdf): retrieval benefited later comprehension and inference on studied science texts relative to the tested comparison conditions. This is not a trial of this calculus schedule.
- [Cepeda et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/): spacing depends on the retention horizon. It does not establish a universal 1/3/7-day prescription.

Caption review establishes what the available text says. It does not verify every spoken claim, original audio, diagram or linked paper. Treat numerical coaching promises, permanent-memory claims and learner percentile labels as unestablished unless independently supported for the relevant task.
